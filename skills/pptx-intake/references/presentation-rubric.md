# Presentation Narrative Rubric

Use this as a revision checklist before finalizing the intake package for `pptx-design`.

## 1. Strategic Clarity

Question: Is the point of the deck obvious?

Strong intake signs:

- the main message is clear fast
- the audience action or decision is explicit
- the "so what" is unmistakable

Weak intake signs:

- buried lead
- vague value
- overly technical framing

## 2. Audience Alignment

Question: Is the narrative built for the intended audience?

Strong intake signs:

- reflects audience priorities, risks, and success measures
- uses the audience's language when supplied
- anticipates likely questions or objections

Weak intake signs:

- sounds reusable for any audience
- talks mostly about West Monroe or the solution

## 3. Compelling Case For Change

Question: Why does this matter now?

Strong intake signs:

- states the stakes and cost of inaction
- makes the current state or opportunity clear
- shows urgency without hype

Weak intake signs:

- jumps to solution before proving need
- makes change feel optional

## 4. Differentiation And Positioning

Question: What makes the recommended direction credible?

Strong intake signs:

- includes specific, supported differentiators
- ties West Monroe's role to the audience's situation
- avoids generic capability language

Weak intake signs:

- relies on "deep expertise" or "best in class"
- lacks proof or specificity

## 5. Logical Flow And Story Arc

Question: Can this become a clear presentation story?

Target arc:

- Situation
- Tension or opportunity
- Insight
- Recommended direction
- Why West Monroe or why this approach
- Proof
- Action or decision

Weak intake signs:

- reads like a list of facts
- no buildup or momentum
- no clear close

## 6. Proof And Credibility

Question: Can the audience believe it?

Strong intake signs:

- relevant examples
- quantified impact where supported
- claims linked to source evidence

Weak intake signs:

- bold claims without proof
- proof not connected to the narrative

## 7. Executive Presence

Question: Does the copy sound confident and presentation-ready?

Strong intake signs:

- direct language
- clear recommendations or implications
- concise phrasing

Weak intake signs:

- hedging
- passive phrasing
- jargon or filler
